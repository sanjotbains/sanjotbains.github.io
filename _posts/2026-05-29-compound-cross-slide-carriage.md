On the typical manual lathe, there are 4 axes that move cutting tools relative to the workpiece-- the compound atop the cross slide atop the carriage, all of course atop the ways, and the endstock on its own. All of these are kind of the same thing, pointed in different directions, gear or screw driven bodies moving very linearly.

<!-- Still need to make this one. -->

Anyhow, all of these can be freed from the rest of the lathe fairly easily so we're able to get them clean thoroughly on the bench without having to contort ourselves too greatly. We'll start with the compound. 

# Compound Axis

<!-- Original mounted compound -->

Turning over the compound, we can first observe the wear in the pivot mating surfaces, as well as the the chip/grease buildup. The wipers are an especially horrible sight, so packed that they only serve to worsen the wear.

![Dirty Compound Bottom]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/compound/dirty_bottom.jpg)

Disassembly starts with loosening the set screws holding the gib and the depth adjustments screws.

![Gib Set Screws]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/compound/gib_setscrews.jpg)
![Gib Adjustment Screws]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/compound/gib_adjustment_screws.jpg)

The nut is held into the boss with a single hex cap screw through the bottom of the casting.

![Nut Hold-Down Screw]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/compound/nut_screw.jpg)

With the gib out and the nut free, the two halves slide apart easily. 

![Compound Halves Separated]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/compound/separated.jpg)

The handle set screw and end screw are removed; the handle slides off the sleeve; the sleeve slides off the lead screw shaft.

![Handle Removed]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/compound/handle_removal.jpg)

Now it gets a bit trickier. I stared at this for quite a while trying to figure out how it comes apart. The only clue is the hole at the bottom, which I at first confused for a really jammed up set screw hole.

The answer is really obvious, actually, assuming one has a hook spanner, and has used it. I had to crawl the forums a bit to arrive at this incredibly obvious answer; I do not have a hook spanner and have never used one; I may have had a few drinks. I considered the drift and hammer like I am often want to do for hydraulic cylinders, but preserving the surface finish is important to me. So naturally I just wrenched it really hard with some channel locks. The grooves left by the jaws should be shallow enough to disappear after an appointment with the Scotchbrite.

![Screw Bearing]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/compound/screw_bearing.jpg)

With the bearing free, the screw and nut can also be separated.

After a bath in the degreaser, and another bath in the Evaporust, and a good scrubbing, we are ready for reassembly, complete with the toolpost I cleaned up last month.

![Compound with Toolpost]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/compound/compund_toolpost.jpg)
![Compound with Toolpost]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/compound/compound_toolpost1.jpg)

# Cross-Slide and Carriage

The cross-slide and carriage follow much the same principles.

![Original Cross-Slide and Carriage]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/cross_slide_original.jpg)

We begin be removing the gib and freeing the lead nut from the slide.

![Gib and Nut Removed]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/gib_and_nut_removed.jpg)

The slide comes off, as well as the handle and dial.

![Removing Handle and Dial]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/removing_handle_and_dial.jpg)

The thrust bearings come out of the bushing.

![Removing Thrust Bearings]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/removing_thrust_bearings.jpg)

The bushing is unscrewed from the carriage.

![Removing Screw Bushing]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/removing_screw_bearing.jpg)

The screw unthreads and comes out of the same hole.

![Screw Removal]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/screw_removal.jpg)

Here we can see the worn nut we semi-resolved in an [earlier article]({{ site.baseurl }}/2026/05/27/lathe-cross-slide-nut-1.html)

![Worn Nut]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/worn_nut.jpg)

To remove the carriage from the ways, we undo the bolt holding the carriage to the apron controls and the rear clamping gib.

![Removing the Rear Gib]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/removing_rear_gib.jpg)

The carriage and the rest of the parts get the same spa treatment as everything else.

![Cleaned Carriage]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/carriage_cleaned.jpg)

With the carriage cleaned, we can see the wear in the slide ways, the contrast of the rut is quite great with the original flaking next to it. 

![Worn Cross-Slide Ways]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/worn_cross_slide_ways.jpg)

All prepped, we can reassemble everything to get it off the bench.

![Reassembled Cross-Slide and Carriage]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/assembled1.jpg)
![Reassembled Cross-Slide and Carriage]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/assembled2.jpg)

I've left off the indexer and the rear gib for the final assembly.

![Thread Indexer]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/thread_indexer.jpg)
![Thread Indexer Exploded]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/sheldon/cross_slide/thread_indexer_exploded.jpg)
