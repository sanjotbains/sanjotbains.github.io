In the past two months, I've done quite a bit of cleaning. I don't know whether the old grease is a boon or a curse; certainly it has kept many of the surfaces rust free, but it might have been easier to clean off the rust. 

In the [headstock gear article]({{ site.baseurl }}/2026/04/09/sheldon-lathe-headstock-gear.html), you'll note the cleaned gear train and covers for the spindle internals, all fairly simple.

I then tackled the [four jaw chuck]({{ site.baseurl }}/2026/04/25/cushman-four-jaw.html), breaking only a single worm gear bushing. The rest of it turned out pretty nice though.

<!-- Cleaned Four Jaw -->

The [toolpost]({{ site.baseurl }}/2026/04/25/toolpost.html) was a quick one. It spent longer in the Evaporust than on the bench. Unfortunately I lost the the handle knob somewhere along the way. I haven't quite decided whether to make the replacement out of wood, metal, or plastic.

<!-- Toolpost Before and After -->

I then started on the [three jaw]({{ site.baseurl }}/2026/05/19/cushman-three-jaw.html), a scroll chuck. This one was a great success, if a bit annoying to get into all the crevices; it all went back together without anything breaking or going missing.

<!-- Three Jaw Chuck Before and After -->

The compound angle axis(?) was also simple, a trial for the other axes.

<!-- Compound Axis -->

I took a detour to the endstock, another easy tuneup.

<!-- Endstock -->

Thereafter, we come back to ride the cross-slide and carriage, where we discovered the worn nut. Freed from the lathe bed, de-rusted and degreased, these make for quite the pretty pieces.

<!-- All stacked atop carriage -->

Next to come off is the apron, a rather messy affair, but largely without issue. How I'll get all these parts clean and back together is a problem for a different man (Heraclitus).

<!-- Apron Assembly -->

And the final calamity. I could not free the leadscrew from the gear selector so they had to come off together.

<!-- Leadscrew and Gearbox -->

Obviously, the gearbox is much heavier than the bare leadscrew and attendant transmission worm gear. Both sides are pinned to the lathe bed, requiring some prying to free. Probably because of the constant oil drip on the pins holding the gearbox, it came free easily. The far end was more difficult. When it came loose, the gearbox pitched off its perch on the pins and crashed to the floor. The casualties include the coupler from the lead screw to the gear box (output) and the shaft that transmits the gear train motion to the gearbox (input).

<!-- Input and Output Shaft -->
> I messed with it some before photographing, so I've drawn in the original bend.

The input shaft is probably serviceable, or at least repairable. The output shaft is definitely not. 

I'm not quite sure how I'll remake this, especially the threaded portion, without a functioning leadscrew. I'm considering cutting off the bent portion and drilling-and-tapping the end to accept a new piece. The female portion is easy enough with a tap, and I can thread the other end with a die fairly easier. 

>Edit: The shaft is fairly soft, likely just mild steel. I stuck the bent end in the vise and managed to bend it fairly straight, at least straight enough to inspire some confidence in my ability to just turn, sleeve, and turn the end back to proper diameter. It's fairly obvious that the threaded portion has some bend as well, but this should be negligible once assembled. Turning the end will produce a concentric and true coupling surface. The threaded portion exists to adjust endplay. 

I suppose there are more elegant solutions, something involving press fits, but I don't really have the headspace for that right now.

That is all to say, we will be putting these irons on the back burner.
